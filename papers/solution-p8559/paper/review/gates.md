# Gates

What only a person can confirm about this paper. Each gate is one line ending
in `open` or `complete`; whatever is worth recording goes on indented lines
beneath. A gate turns to `complete` only when the thing itself was done.

- Self-contained argument: complete
  All nine compiled pages read against the checklist. Every step is on the
  page: the eleven monomials are classified in a printed table, and the two
  dimension counts are the partition numbers, stated in the text. The one
  program that exists checks a transcription, and a remark says so and says
  the proof does not rest on it.

- Literature survey: complete
  Co-citation over the three fetched sources was run both unrestricted and
  restricted to their introductions; the union supplied the founding band
  (Belavin--Polyakov--Zamolodchikov; the hidden Grassmann structure series)
  and the current band. The Virasoro representation-theory band, which no
  source in this subject cites and no keyword search returns, was added
  deliberately. All four bands are covered in 26 entries. Co-citation still
  reports works two or more sources cite and this one does not:
  Destri--de Vega, Baxter's book, Klumper and collaborators, Boos--Gohmann,
  Dotsenko--Fateev, and further XXZ and thermodynamic-Bethe-ansatz
  machinery. Those are the apparatus of the fermionic-basis calculations,
  not of this argument, which uses a commutator identity and two partition
  numbers; they are omitted deliberately rather than overlooked.

- Citation check: complete
  The run exits clean. 46 attributions over 15 citing paragraphs; 32 rest

- Source convention at $m=0$ confirmed with the authors: open
  The counterexample rests on reading the assertion literally at its
  endpoint: "for every $m\ge0$" includes $m=0$, where the primary is the
  identity operator with no limiting renormalization, so every
  $\mathcal I_{2k-1}\phi_0=[I_{2k-1},1]=0$, eight of the eleven monomials
  vanish, and the span drops to dimension at most three against Virasoro
  targets of eleven and four. Ask Boos and Smirnov which they intended:

  > (a) the identity sector is included as written -- the note refutes their
  > stated assertion completely, and is strongest;
  > (b) they meant to exclude $m=0$ -- the note instead shows a hypothesis is
  > missing, and $m\ge1$ stays open;
  > (c) they meant a renormalized $m\to0$ limit -- same as (b).

  None of the three retracts the counterexample to the statement as written,
  so this changes how the paper is positioned, not whether it is correct.
  Failing it means rewriting the framing in the introduction and \S5, not the
  mathematics.
